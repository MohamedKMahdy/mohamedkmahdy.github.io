How I build

A few things I care about

The same handful of rules shows up in almost everything above.

01

People stay in control

Every action is one undo step, a dry run comes before anything changes, and when AI proposes something, a person approves it.

02

Boring is a feature

Tools that run overnight on their own, try again when the network drops, and never leave a half-finished result that looks complete.

03

Data stays where it belongs

AI models that run locally, IFC files read in the browser instead of uploaded, and no passwords or keys built into a program.

04

Open where it counts

Open file formats such as IFC, IDS and BCF wherever they fit, so a workflow is not locked to one vendor.

About

A bit about me

I trained as an architectural engineer and moved into software because the most interesting problems in construction are data problems. Most firms have valuable project data trapped in disconnected tools and manual workflows. I build the systems that connect it.

That has meant Revit and Navisworks plugins, model-health and clash-analytics platforms, and enterprise automation, across Cairo, Dubai and Munich. Along the way I completed 18 freelance client projects over 895 billable hours with a 5.0 average rating, and doubled my rate over that time.

My M.Sc. at TUM added the half I was missing: graduate courses in geographic information science, spatial data mining, photogrammetry and remote sensing, alongside semantic modelling and software engineering. BIM, GIS and AI are converging, and very few people work across all three.

I speak Arabic natively, English at a professional working level (IELTS Academic 6.5), and I am learning German (A2).
