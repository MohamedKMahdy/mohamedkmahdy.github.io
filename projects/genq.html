GenQ

A priced bill and a cost plan without leaving Revit.

  • C#
  • Revit API 2022 to 2027
  • .NET Framework 4.8 / .NET 8 / .NET 10
  • ClosedXML
  • PDFsharp
  • GAEB DA XML 3.3
  • xUnit

The problem

Quantity surveyors often re-measure what the model already knows. GenQ reads the quantities straight from Revit, prices them and turns them into a bill and a cost plan.

What it does

Tick the element types to price and give each one a unit, a bill section (CSI MasterFormat 2018, RICS NRM2 or your own list), an element for the cost plan (UniFormat, RICS NRM1, DIN 276 or your own list), a rate and a description. GenQ suggests all of these from your earlier projects, Revit keynotes and assembly codes, and each type's name and materials, with the reason for each; you check them before anything changes.

It then measures the model and gives you the bill by trade and the cost plan by element, with cost per m² of gross floor area and additions such as preliminaries, contingency and VAT. Exports go to Excel, PDF and CSV, and to GAEB DA XML for German tendering and costing software, validated against the official schemas. Elements are written back into Revit's Assembly Code, so schedules and the IFC export carry them.

Under the hood it measures the model in one pass, filters by phase, design option and level, counts repeated linked models, and matches categories by Revit's built-in ids, so it works in any Revit language. Every third-party library is bundled into one assembly, so GenQ never clashes with Revit's own copies or other add-ins. Version 3.1 also absorbed the separate UniFormat Code Manager add-in.

HighlightSelf-tested inside Revit on Autodesk's Snowdon Towers sample: 392 types, 294 suggestions, 136 bill lines, a cost plan by element, and a GAEB export that validates against the official 3.3 schemas.
