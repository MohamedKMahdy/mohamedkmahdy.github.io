Qubecta

A priced bill and a cost plan without leaving Revit.

  • C#
  • Revit API 2022 to 2027
  • .NET Framework 4.8 / .NET 8 / .NET 10
  • ClosedXML
  • PDFsharp
  • GAEB DA XML 3.3
  • xUnit

The problem

Quantity surveyors often re-measure what the model already knows. Qubecta reads the quantities straight from Revit, prices them and turns them into a bill and a cost plan.

What it does

Qubecta (QUantities, Bills, Elements, Costs, Tendering and Assembly codes) is an add-in for Revit 2022 to 2027. Tick the element types to price and give each one a unit, a bill section (CSI MasterFormat 2018, RICS NRM2 or your own list), an element for the cost plan (UniFormat, RICS NRM1, DIN 276 or your own list), a rate and a description. Qubecta suggests all of these from your earlier projects, Revit keynotes and assembly codes, and each type's name and materials, with the reason for each; you check them before anything changes.

It then measures the model and gives you the bill by trade and the cost plan by element, with cost per m² of gross floor area and additions such as preliminaries, contingency and VAT. Exports go to Excel, PDF and CSV, and to GAEB DA XML for German tendering and costing software, validated against the official schemas. Elements are written back into Revit's Assembly Code, so schedules and the IFC export carry them.

Under the hood it measures the model in one pass, filters by phase, design option and level, counts repeated linked models, and matches categories by Revit's built-in ids, so it works in any Revit language. Every third-party library is bundled into one assembly, so Qubecta never clashes with Revit's own copies or other add-ins. Elements are also read from where things are: doors and windows take theirs from the wall or roof they sit in, and walls and floors from their exterior and load-bearing settings.

Features

  • Units, bill sections, cost plan elements, rates and descriptions per element type
  • Code lists: CSI MasterFormat 2018, RICS NRM2 and NRM1, UniFormat II and DIN 276 built in; Uniclass 2015, OmniClass and Revit assembly code files read as they are
  • Suggestions from earlier projects, Revit keynotes and assembly codes, and each type's name and materials, each with its reason
  • Elements from where things are: doors and windows from their host, walls and floors from exterior and load-bearing settings
  • Measures count, length, area, volume, perimeter, weight from material density, and one line per material layer
  • Filters by phase, design option and level; counts repeated placements of linked models
  • Bill by trade and cost plan by element, with cost per m² of gross floor area
  • Additions on the running total: preliminaries, overheads and profit, contingency and VAT; waste percentage; items not in the model
  • Exports to Excel, PDF and CSV, and to GAEB DA XML 3.3 (X83 tender, X82 priced estimate) validated against the official schemas
  • Writes elements back into Revit's Assembly Code, so schedules and the IFC export carry them
  • Settings and rates are saved in the model; price lists move between projects as Excel files
  • Works in any Revit language; one docked panel for Revit 2022 to 2027
HighlightSelf-tested inside Revit on Autodesk's Snowdon Towers sample: 392 types, 294 suggestions, 136 bill lines, a cost plan by element, and a GAEB export that validates against the official 3.3 schemas.
